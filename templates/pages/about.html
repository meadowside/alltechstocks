{{ site.name }} shows which US and UK listed tech companies the news is covering most, updated every hour. It's a place to start your research. It never tells you what to buy or sell.

How it works

Every hour we check about 45 news sources: Reuters, Bloomberg, the FT, the Wall Street Journal, CNBC and the rest of the US and UK financial press, plus tech titles, company newsrooms and London Stock Exchange announcements. We also run news searches covering all {{ stocks|length }} companies we follow, a few at a time. To decide what counts as a tech stock we use the official classifications: for the US, companies in the Information Technology and Communication Services sectors of the standard used by the S&P 500; for the UK, companies based in the UK with their main listing in London that the London Stock Exchange files under Technology or Telecommunications. When several outlets report the same event, we group their articles into one story.

Stories are ranked by how many outlets are covering them. A major financial paper counts for more than a specialist title. A story has to name a US or UK listed tech company we follow to appear, unless it's about tech shares as a whole and widely covered (two national papers, say). Older stories lose half their score every 12 hours, so the top of the page shows what's being covered right now. The stock table works the same way. Nobody picks the order by hand.

Every headline links straight to the publisher that wrote it. We don't copy or rewrite articles.

Filings and prices

For US companies we also list filings from the SEC's free EDGAR service: results, deals, insider trades and other official documents. UK companies publish through the London Stock Exchange, and those announcements appear when outlets cover them.

Price charts come from TradingView's free widgets. They're delayed and load only when you allow them. For anything you'll act on, use your broker's own prices.

What this site isn't

A lot of coverage means a company is being talked about. It doesn't mean its shares will go up, down or anywhere. We never publish tips, top picks, price targets or predictions, and we filter out other people's. Read why this isn't advice.

The "So what" lines

The short "So what" line under a story is written by AI from the coverage. It explains what the news means for the company's business and is checked by software to stop it commenting on the shares. Like any summary it can be wrong. The publisher's article is always the source to trust.
