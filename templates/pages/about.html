{{ site.name }} shows which US and UK listed tech companies the news is covering most, updated every hour. It's a place to start your research. It never tells you what to buy or sell.

How it works

Every hour we check about 45 news sources: Reuters, Bloomberg, the FT, the Wall Street Journal, CNBC and the rest of the US and UK financial press, plus tech titles, company newsrooms and London Stock Exchange announcements. We also run news searches covering all {{ stocks|length }} companies we follow, a few at a time. We follow US and UK companies: a company counts as US or UK by where it's headquartered, and it must be listed in New York or London. To decide what counts as tech we use the official classifications: for the US, the Information Technology and Communication Services sectors of the standard used by the S&P 500; for the UK, the companies the London Stock Exchange files under Technology or Telecommunications. When several outlets report the same event, we group their articles into one story.

Stories are ranked by how many outlets are covering them. A major financial paper counts for more than a specialist title. A story has to name a US or UK listed tech company we follow to appear, unless it's about tech shares as a whole and widely covered (two national papers, say). Older stories lose half their score every 12 hours, so the top of the page shows what's being covered right now. The stock table works the same way. Nobody picks the order by hand.

Every headline links straight to the publisher that wrote it. We don't copy or rewrite articles.

Filings and prices

For US companies we also list filings from the SEC's free EDGAR service: results, deals, insider trades and other official documents. UK companies publish through the London Stock Exchange, and those announcements appear when outlets cover them.

Price charts come from TradingView's free widgets. They're delayed and load only when you allow them. For anything you'll act on, use your broker's own prices.

What this site isn't

A lot of coverage means a company is being talked about. It doesn't mean its shares will go up, down or anywhere. We never publish tips, top picks, price targets or predictions, and we filter out other people's. Read why this isn't advice.

Questions

What is All Tech Stocks?

A news site that ranks stories about US and UK listed tech companies by how many outlets are covering them. It reads the major financial papers, markets titles and tech press every hour and links every headline to the publisher.

How are stories ranked?

By coverage. Each outlet covering a story counts once, and major financial papers such as Reuters, Bloomberg and the FT count for more. Fresh stories rank above older ones. Nobody picks the order by hand.

Which stocks does it follow?

About 125 tech companies headquartered in the US or UK and listed in New York or London, from Apple and Nvidia to Arm and Sage. Each has its own page, and every Monday we publish a ranking of the most covered.

Is anything here financial advice?

No. A lot of coverage means a company is being talked about, not that its shares will rise or fall. We never publish tips, price targets or predictions.

How often is it updated?

Every hour, around the clock, seven days a week.
